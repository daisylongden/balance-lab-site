Balance Lab / How we use AI

How we use AI, and where we won't

We help mission-driven organisations use AI to free up time for their mission. This page sets out the rules we hold ourselves to, so you can decide if our way of doing things fits yours.

Our stanceWhat we stand forRed linesCommitmentsYour own AI policyQuestions

We can't erase all of AI's harmful impact. We can keep it as low as possible.

The way we work with AI is designed to reduce its harm as far as we can, while still giving mission-driven organisations the chance to do more with their time.

Striking that balance, between helping the people making a difference do more and limiting the harm AI causes, is something we navigate every day.

We also fully respect organisations that decide not to use AI at all.

What we stand for

Four values behind every build

Jobs

Capacity, not cuts

We free up time for the work only people can do. We don't take on projects designed to replace your team.

People

People decide about people

AI can draft, summarise and sort. Decisions about who gets help stay with humans, and anyone talking to an AI can reach a person.

Environment

Use only what's needed

We pick the smallest tool that does the job, and avoid AI where simpler fixes work.

Independence

Yours to keep

Your data stays protected, your rules stay written down, and your tools stay portable. We take no fees from AI vendors, so our advice is independent.

Red lines

What we won't build, for anyone

These are not negotiable, whatever the budget. If a project needs one of them, we'll say no and explain why.

Scoring or ranking people

No tools that decide eligibility, risk, fraud or priority of support. The Dutch childcare benefits scandal showed what that can do to families.

Emotion recognition

No reading the emotions of staff, volunteers or the people you serve.

Unsupervised crisis support

No chatbots for mental health, abuse or crisis without a human handover.

Made-up people

No generated images or stories presented as real people.

Hidden AI

No tools that talk to people without saying they are AI.

Replacement projects

No builds where the main goal is cutting jobs.

Commitments

Nine things we do on every project

Each one comes with a check you can ask to see. We report on them in every results report, alongside the hours saved.

Environment

Right-size every build

The smallest model that does the job. No AI where a rule, template or spreadsheet formula works. No generated images or video unless you ask for them.

You'll see: the smallest setup that does the job, written down in your handover pack.

Fairness

Keep AI out of decisions about people

AI drafts, summarises and handles routine questions. Where a build touches people's requests, we test it with a varied sample, in every language you work in, with staff who know the community.

You'll see: the test sample and what we changed.

Data

Data classes before tools

In week 1 we sort your data into public, internal, personal and sensitive, and agree which tools may touch each. Personal data only goes into business-tier tools with a data processing agreement and no training on inputs.

You'll see: a one-page data map, and a flag if you need a data protection impact assessment (DPIA).

Jobs

Capacity, not cuts

The people who do the work co-design the change. Where a tool could monitor staff, we check whether works council consent is needed.

You'll see: time saved reported next to what the team will do with it.

Dignity

A human route, always

Anyone talking to an AI tool is told so and can reach a person. Public-facing tools hand crisis, health and legal questions to staff.

You'll see: the tested list of topics the tool hands over.

Accuracy

A named person signs off

Anything that leaves your organisation, such as funder reports, donor messages or translations, has a named reviewer built into the workflow.

You'll see: whether sign-off still happens, checked at month 3.

Independence

Portable builds, no vendor fees

We take no referral fees or commissions from AI vendors. Prompts, workflows and data exports are documented so you can switch provider, and we offer European or open-model options.

You'll see: a handover pack you could give to anyone.

Transparency

We say where we used AI

If we used AI in our own work for you, for example to draft a report or analyse survey answers, we tell you, and a person on our side checks it.

You'll see: a note on each deliverable.

Review

Harm counts in every verdict

Our week 6 and month 3 verdicts of keep, adjust or drop cover harm as well as hours saved. If something isn't safe, we recommend dropping it, even if it saves time.

You'll see: incidents, complaints and red flags in the results report.

Questions we hear

About AI and ethics

Will you ever say no?

Yes. We won't build tools that score or rank people for support, hide that they are AI, or exist mainly to cut jobs. Sometimes the best fix isn't AI at all, and we'll say so.

What if we're not sure AI fits who we are?

That's a reasonable place to start. We can begin with your red lines and the smallest, lowest-risk use, and "not now" is always an acceptable answer.

Which AI tools do you use?

Whatever fits your data, budget and values, including European and open options. We take no commissions, and everything we build can move with you.

Our data is sensitive. Can we still work with you?

Yes. We start with workflows that don't touch sensitive data, and put rules and contracts in place before anything that does.

Our donors might not like it.

Hidden use is the bigger risk. We help you write a short public AI statement and keep AI away from decisions about people, where public support is lowest.

Does this cover the EU AI Act?

Our training helps you meet the AI literacy duty for staff, and our rules on telling people when they talk to AI match the Act's transparency duties. We're not lawyers, and we'll tell you when you need one.
